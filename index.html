About

I'm a sophomore at MIT studying computer science and molecular biology. I'm interested in machine learning for protein engineering: predicting what a mutation will do to a protein, and then using those predictions to design better ones.

Outside of academics, I row in the first varsity eight on MIT's lightweight crew team. I grew up in Cambridge and started rowing at Cambridge Rindge and Latin, where I captained the team.

Now

Working on ProteinGym2 in the Marks Lab, and on Aurora, a side project designing GFP variants with protein language models.

Research

Marks Lab, Harvard Medical School 2026 – now

I lead the supervised track of ProteinGym2, a benchmark for predicting the effects of protein mutations. Most of my work is on how well models trained on some proteins transfer to ones they've never seen, and on making sure the evaluation actually measures that.

Hong Lab, MIT Chemistry 2025 – 2026

I expressed and purified the envelope (E) protein of the MERS coronavirus for NMR structural studies, and ran experiments on the lab's 800 MHz spectrometer.

Projects

Aurora 2026 – now

Fine-tuning ESM-2 to predict GFP fluorescence, then using it to design new variants. The interesting part has been catching the model fooling itself, proposing sequences it loves that are far from anything it has seen. A trust region fixed most of that.

Syndesis Health Summer 2026

Software engineering internship. I shipped two production apps that replaced a spreadsheet process for coordinating health-data requests across 61 data partners.

Rowdar 2024 – 2025

An onboard tracking system for rowing crews and coaches, built in high school with a Lemelson-MIT InvenTeam grant. We filed a provisional patent.
